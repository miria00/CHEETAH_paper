\section{Conditioning of the Comprehensive Program: The Case for Variable-Metric Rescaling}
\label{app:socp_conditioning}

\emph{This appendix records a preliminary numerical finding, noted here for further
discussion and to motivate the solver of Section~\ref{sec:solver_vm} as a
contribution in its own right.}

Assembling the comprehensive CHEETAH-V1 program (\cref{app:master_v3}) as a single
monolithic conic program for DeepSeek-V3 -- with all decisions as decision variables
and the hardware reciprocals as exact rotated second-order cones -- yields a
compact but severely ill-conditioned instance ($11{,}599$ variables, $32{,}582$
inequality rows, two exact SOC cones, and the McCormick envelopes for the genuine
bilinears). In natural units the coefficient dynamic range spans roughly
$3\times10^{27}$, dominated by the big-$M$ constants ($\sim\!10^{18}$) in the
indicator and capacity rows that link the discrete residency and one-hot selections
to the continuous schedule.

At this conditioning, \emph{off-the-shelf conic solvers do not solve the program.}
An interior-point conic solver (Clarabel) reports a spurious \texttt{unbounded}
status; a second interior-point solver (ECOS) raises a numerical exception; and a
first-order solver (SCS) returns bound-violating iterates (for example a negative
provisioned bandwidth) even after $10^5$ iterations. A simple \emph{exact} diagonal
unit rescaling -- expressing latency in units of $10^{9}$ cycles and traffic in
units of $10^{6}$ bytes, which leaves the optimum unchanged -- compresses the
dynamic range to $\sim\!4.5\times10^{15}$ and restores solvability: Clarabel then
returns an \texttt{optimal} solution in about $0.6$ seconds. Even after rescaling
the program remains \emph{stiff}: the interior-point method intermittently stalls at
suboptimal points that it nonetheless flags \texttt{optimal}, so a multi-start (a
mild perturbation of the compute floor) is required to certify the true optimum.

This is precisely the regime that the VM-One-Armed-Rescue solver of
Section~\ref{sec:solver_vm} is designed for: a restarted primal-dual hybrid gradient
method with a \emph{diagonal variable-metric rescaling} and a one-sided rescue
restart. The finding above is direct evidence that the comprehensive co-design
program, once every decision and the hardware reciprocals are folded in, is not
reliably solvable by stock conic solvers without such rescaling and restart
machinery. We view this as strengthening the case for VM-One-Armed-Rescue as a
contribution: the more expressive the co-design program becomes, the more its
conditioning, rather than its size, is the binding obstacle, and the more a
metric-aware first-order solver is required to make it tractable. A full
solver-versus-solver comparison at this scale is left as immediate future work.
