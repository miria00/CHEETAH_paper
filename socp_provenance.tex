\section{Audits, Solver, and Serving Validation}
\label{app:lp_provenance}

\emph{This appendix maps the comprehensive program's constraint families to the
production systems they are drawn from, states which solver solves the resulting
linear program, and reports a preliminary serving-regime validation on
DeepSeek-V3. The results here are at the level of the LP relaxation (menu
selectors relaxed) and are calibrated on the analytic roofline; every
percentage is therefore a relaxation-level estimate, certified in neither
direction and produced before the copy-bound fix of \eqref{eq:v3_arrayshape},
and certified $(\mathrm{LB},\mathrm{UB})$ pairs from pinned-selector solves
replace them; those certified pairs are now measured and are collected in
\cref{tab:lever_pairs}, and the certified sandwich for the serving program with
its full lever set is reported at the end of this appendix.}

\paragraph{What we fold in, and from where.}
The constraint families of \cref{sec:v3_levers,app:master_v3} are not invented in a
vacuum: each is a technique from a production serving or kernel system, re-expressed
as a certified constraint family whose only effect is on bytes moved, FLOPs,
on-chip residency, or a hardware parameter. \Cref{tab:provenance} lists the mapping,
together with whether the decision binds on DeepSeek-V3 \emph{serving}.

\paragraph{How the mapping works: coefficients as functions of the hardware.}
The single modelling principle behind the table is that the \emph{only} constants
are the reference per-tile Pareto bases ($\Tmin(c)$, $\Tmax(c)$, byte footprints);
every hardware coefficient is written as $\text{base}\times f(\text{decision
variables})$. Compute time is $\sum_{a,f}\Tmin(c)\,v^{a,f}/(\eta_{a}\gamma_f)$ over
per-branch copies of $v$, and memory time is the bandwidth-menu disaggregation of
the (precision-, cache-, and eviction-scaled) bytes; $v\ge 1/m$ is bracketed by
tangent and chord rows, and $u$ is the exact menu value, so every product is
exact at integral selectors. The classical per-trial Pareto rescale thus becomes
an in-program function of the hardware variables rather than a per-chip
precompute. Instantiated for DeepSeek-V3 and shape-aggregated ($903$ operations
$\to 13$ unique shapes, \cref{app:shape_agg}), the program has
$6{,}815$ variables and $510$ equality plus $22{,}511$ inequality rows, all
linear, to which the exported instances add $89$ tangent (or $88$ chord) rows
for the compute reciprocal ($23{,}110$ rows and $58{,}698$ nonzeros for the
certificate instance); the bandwidth menu has $8$ points (the $8$ of the $12$
catalogue points that pass the pin gate $\mathrm{BW}/0.8\le 4096$ pins), and
hull rows for every selector-times-quantity product, compact, versus
${\sim}8.7$M rows for the naive per-operation build.

\paragraph{Which solver.}
The program is a linear program. The shape-aggregated instances are solved by
HiGHS (certified vertex, $0.05$ seconds per instance) and by CORGI, our
restarted primal-dual hybrid gradient method with variable-metric rescaling
(\cref{sec:solver_vm}), which is intended for the full-resolution instances
(\todo{measured: CORGI and HiGHS on the full-resolution instance}). On the $67$
exported serving instances ($32$ pinned
bandwidth-times-decode-precision branches, each as an outer and a chord LP,
plus the relaxed root pair and one diagnostic) the two do not yet agree: HiGHS
returns an optimal vertex on all $67$, while CORGI, at its $200{,}000$-iteration
budget (about $35$ seconds per instance on an RTX 5090), does not meet its own
convergence test on any of them. On the pinned branch LPs its primal objective
differs from the HiGHS optimum by between $3.7\times10^{-3}$ and $18.5$
relative (median $3.4$; only the $225$ GB/s branch is within $0.3$), and on the
fully relaxed root LPs it stops $1.8$ to $2.7$ times above the optimum. The
relaxed root is measurably degenerate (at the HiGHS optimum only $122$ of the
$6{,}815$ columns are basic, $5{,}971$ non-basic columns have zero reduced
cost, and $17{,}430$ basic rows are tight), which is the cause
(\cref{app:socp_conditioning}); the GPU run is also not deterministic
from run to run, and the $10^{30}$ row-bound sentinels of the export leak into
CORGI's dual objective, so no per-run gap value is reproducible. Every bound
reported here is therefore HiGHS's.
As \cref{app:socp_conditioning} details, the earlier conic form of the same
program was too ill-conditioned in natural units for off-the-shelf conic solvers
to solve at all, and stiff even after rescaling; the linear form removes the
big-$M$ constants that dominated that range. The lower bounds we report are
safe dual bounds of the outer (tangent) LP: the larger of the root bound (all
menu selectors relaxed) and the disjunctive bound (the minimum, over the
enumerated bandwidth-times-decode-precision branches, of the branch outer LP's
safe dual bound). The achievable designs come from the chord LP with every
selector pinned and verified by an exact-lift check.

\paragraph{A preliminary serving validation on DeepSeek-V3.}
The decisions are memory-regime decisions, so the objective the program is written over
determines whether they bind. Under a \emph{prefill}-latency objective ($\min\sum_i
T_i$) the DeepSeek-V3 optimum is compute-bound (it provisions compute to the reticle
limit), and all of the candidate decisions are inert (they move the objective by less
than $0.006\%$). Under the \emph{serving}-latency objective
$\min\,T_{\mathrm{prefill}}+G\cdot T_{\mathrm{decode}}$ (context $4096$, batch $32$,
$G=512$), where the memory-bound decode phase dominates by construction, the decisions
activate. Each lever set is now solved to a certified pair: the lower bound is
the larger of the safe dual bound of the root outer LP and the smallest safe
dual bound over the complete pinned-selector enumeration, and the upper bound is
the best exact-lifted pinned-selector design, so
$1-\mathrm{UB}_{\mathrm{lever}}/\mathrm{LB}_{\mathrm{base}}$ is a certified
improvement and $1-\mathrm{LB}_{\mathrm{lever}}/\mathrm{UB}_{\mathrm{base}}$
an optimistic one. \Cref{tab:lever_pairs} reports them against a base
configuration with every lever off (W8A8 weights and activations, an 8-bit KV
cache, dense experts, no expert pinning or host tier, HBM, full context, no
prefix cache, no register-file residency and no overlap). W4 weight precision
alone is certified to remove at least $45.1\%$ of serving latency and $2{:}4$
sparsity at least $42.0\%$; both act on the mixture-of-experts weight reload,
which is ${\sim}98\%$ of decode DRAM traffic. The full lever set is certified at
$65.5\%$, but its interval $[18.83,\,22.02]$ overlaps the interval
$[18.81,\,21.66]$ of W4 and $2{:}4$ sparsity together, so the remaining eleven
levers are certified to improve on that pair in neither direction. The
compute-side ThunderKittens decisions (overlap, register-file occupancy) remain
inert, which is correct for a weight-reload-bound workload, and pinning, memory
technology, the attention split, grouped expert GEMM, prefix caching and the KV
budget move neither bound by more than $10^{-4}$ on this workload. The earlier
relaxation-level estimates (${\approx}28.6\%$ for W4, ${\approx}15.5\%$ for
$2{:}4$) were produced before the copy-bound fix of \eqref{eq:v3_arrayshape} and
compared two relaxed optima, whose ratio bounds the ratio of the true optima in
neither direction; the certified numbers are larger.

\paragraph{Certified sandwich at the solved operating point.}
For the serving program at the same operating point (context $4096$, batch
$32$, $G=512$) with the default lever set enabled and the LP rescaled as in
\cref{app:socp_conditioning} (objective in units of $10^{9}$ cycles at the
$1$\,GHz convention, one cycle per nanosecond), the pinned-selector enumeration over the $8$ bandwidth points and the
$4$ decode-precision options gives $32$ branches, every one of which HiGHS
solves to optimality. The root outer LP (all selectors relaxed, tangent grid
$10^{-3}$) has safe dual bound $\mathrm{LB}_{\mathrm{root}}=16.836$; the
disjunctive bound over the $32$ branch outer LPs on the same grid is
$\mathrm{LB}=18.830$, attained at $3200$ GB/s with W4A8 and a 4-bit KV cache,
and is the certified lower bound. The best exact-lifted design sits in the same
branch: $\mathrm{UB}=22.018$ at $3200$ GB/s with W4A8 and a 4-bit KV cache
(lift residual $5.9\times10^{-17}$, every selector integral), so the certified
pair is $(\mathrm{LB},\mathrm{UB})=(18.830,\,22.018)\times10^{9}$ cycles at
the $1$\,GHz convention, a gap of
$14.5\%$ of the upper bound ($23.5\%$ against the root bound alone). Twenty of
the $32$ branches lift directly; the other twelve fail the argmax rounding of
the memory-technology selector, lift once that selector is pinned to HBM, and
none of them beats the winner (best $25.303$). The in-branch integrality gap of
the winner is $0.145$, of which the reciprocal sandwich contributes
$2.2\times10^{-4}$ and the rounding of the selectors that are not enumerated
contributes $0.145$, so the remaining gap is rounding, not the reciprocal
envelope (the root reciprocal residual is $4.5\times10^{-4}$, inside the
$10^{-3}$ grid). An independent run on an H100 reproduces every HiGHS bound on
these certificate LPs to every printed digit.

\paragraph{A defect these certificates exposed.}
The first certified sandwich we obtained for this program was
$(1.910,\,4.118)\times10^{9}$ cycles, a factor of five better than the numbers
above, and the design behind it served all $256$ experts from the host tier. The
tiered-memory family removes hosted experts from the DRAM reload and defines a
host-link time $T_{\mathrm{host}}$, but the epigraph that charges it,
$T^{\mathrm{step}}_{\mathrm{dec}} \ge T_{\mathrm{host}}$, was missing from the
implementation, so hosting an expert was free and the optimiser hosted every
one: $186$ GB per decode pass over a $64$ GB/s link, charged at nothing. With
the epigraph restored the same enumeration returns the pair reported above and
the winning design hosts $10$ of $256$ experts, worth one to two percent rather
than a factor of sixteen. We report this because it is the failure mode a
certified bound is for: the sandwich itself was valid at every step, and what it
certified was the model, not the machine.
